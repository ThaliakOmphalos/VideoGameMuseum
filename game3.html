Exhibit 03 · Action Puzzle · 2005

Luxor

Chains of colored spheres roll across ancient Egypt toward the pyramids, and only your aim can stop them. Luxor is the definition of "easy to learn, hard to put down."

About the Game

Luxor is an action puzzle game developed and published by MumboJumbo and released in 2005. It was designed by Scott Hansen. The game is set in ancient Egypt, where the god Set threatens the land and the goddess Isis calls on you for help.

In each level, a chain of colored spheres is pushed along a winding path toward a pyramid. You control a winged scarab launcher that slides back and forth along the bottom of the screen. Shoot spheres into the chain so that three or more of the same color touch, and they disappear. If any sphere reaches the pyramid, you lose a life.

Why It Belongs in the Museum

  • A casual gaming classic. It was one of the defining PC "marble popper" games of the 2000s.
  • Power-ups. Falling bonuses like fireballs, lightning, and color bombs can turn a losing level around in seconds.
  • A long-running series. It led to sequels including Luxor: Amun Rising, Luxor 2, Luxor 3, and Luxor Evolved, plus versions for consoles, handhelds, and phones.

Why It's One of My Favorites

Luxor is simple, but it gets intense fast. When the chain speeds up and creeps toward the pyramid, every shot counts. Setting up a big combo that clears half the screen in one chain reaction is extremely satisfying. It's the perfect game for a quick break that turns into an hour.

Nothing beats a last-second combo that saves the pyramid.
